\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly applied to financial tasks, but the dominant evaluation paradigm targets \emph{comprehension} of already-prepared artifacts. FinQA \citep{chen-etal-2021-finqa}, ConvFinQA \citep{chen-etal-2022-convfinqa}, and TAT-QA \citep{zhu-etal-2021-tatqa} pose numerical questions over excerpted tables and text, while DocFinQA \citep{reddy-etal-2024-docfinqa} pairs FinQA-style questions with full SEC reports. FinanceBench \citep{islam2023financebench} and FinAuditing \citep{wang2026finauditing} test analysis or auditing over prepared filings, while FinanceMATH \citep{zhao-etal-2024-financemath} and FinBen \citep{xie2024finben} evaluate broader prepared financial problems and tasks. Across these settings, the model reasons over supplied inputs rather than constructing a ledger and balance sheet from source-document bundles.

Real accounting workflows begin one step earlier. A bundle of source documents---invoices, bank statements, payment notices, contracts, schedules, exemption certificates---must be reconciled into a transaction-level ledger of debit/credit journal entries and a balance sheet: each transaction must be extracted as a balanced entry citing its supporting documents, the entries aggregated into a statement that respects the accounting equation, and contradictions among documents detected and classified rather than silently resolved. We introduce \benchmark{}, a multi-document accounting reconciliation benchmark built from source-document bundles with clean, templated OCR text across eight industries, three period types, and five difficulty levels. Its concept-coverage flags include revenue recognition under ASC~606, leases, deferred tax, asset disposals, multi-jurisdictional sales tax, foreign-exchange scenarios, and tax exemptions; Appendix~\ref{appx:dataset-details} gives the complete list.

\benchmark{} supports both evaluation and diagnostics. Its construction requires explicit models of business processes, evidence documents, accounting policies, document dependencies, distractors, and contradiction types. Performance on clean, fully specified bundles is a controlled diagnostic for real accounting records, which add layout noise, OCR errors, missing evidence, policy choices, and institutional conventions. We evaluate six contemporary-model baselines, citation ablations, context-vs.-logic stress tests, text-emitted and provider-native tool protocols, best-of-3 selection, and forced ledger feedback. In this panel, the highest exact final-balance-sheet score is 46\%, and strict document-grounded end-to-end accuracy remains low.

\benchmark{} is synthetic, with its accounting content specified by the authors. The specification defines business scenarios across industries, document schemas, a chart of accounts, accounting policies, tax and FX treatments, support-document dependencies, distractor templates, contradiction templates, and valid ranges for amounts, dates, counterparties, document roles, and scenario combinations. The generator samples valid combinations, fills document fields, selects compatible evidence and distractors, renders source documents with OCR text, and uses the fixed double-entry ledger to compute the expected journal entries and balance sheet. This process produces controlled records that would be expensive to write individually. Every expected entry, balance sheet, opening trial balance, and inconsistency code remains computable, auditable, and reproducible from the specification.

We report two complementary balance-sheet metrics. \bsexact{} measures whether the model's reported balance sheet matches ground truth exactly. \bsrecon{} instead parses the journal entries produced by the model, applies those entries to the opening balance using our deterministic ledger, and checks whether the resulting balance sheet matches ground truth. The two diverge when the model writes entries with the right accounts and amounts, but its aggregated balance sheet is wrong. Evaluating six contemporary LLMs (Gemini~3 Flash, GPT-5, Claude Haiku~4.5, Grok-4.3, DeepSeek Chat, Qwen~3 235B) yields three findings. \textbf{(i)}~An \aggGap{}: four of six show $26$--$41$\,pp between \bsrecon{} and \bsexact{}. The remaining two lack the gap for qualitatively different reasons: GPT-5 is comparatively internally consistent, while Grok-4.3 has a smaller gap but lower entry recall (\S\ref{sec:analysis}). \textbf{(ii)}~Document-linking is the dominant entry-level failure: on the compact 143-record ablation split, document-reference mismatches affect 52\% of expected postings, a rate that barely moves under explicit citation-pressure prompting ($-1.4$\,pp). The failure concerns selecting the correct supporting documents, not prompt compliance. \textbf{(iii)}~When we use the deterministic ledger as a diagnostic tool and return its replayed balance sheet and account-level deltas to the model, \bsexact{} rises by $+30$ to $+33$\,pp across three model families; the same feedback protocol also reveals a trade-off, degrading inconsistency-code detection by $13$ to $52$\,pp on smaller models.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\textbf{(1)}~A multi-document accounting reconciliation benchmark with deterministic ground truth, 23 inconsistency codes, and a released generator\footnote{Code and data: \url{https://github.com/Devansh1105/finbalance}.} for fresh stratified samples. \textbf{(2)}~A six-model evaluation on 710 records revealing an \aggGap{}, with the highest evaluated model reaching 46\% \bsexact{}, plus failure breakdowns showing which industries, ledger families, concept flags, and error types drive the aggregate scores. \textbf{(3)}~A diagnostic ablation suite covering citation pressure, context stress, text-emitted and provider-native tool use, best-of-3 selection, and forced ledger feedback. \textbf{(4)}~Expert validation by practising finance professionals, including an independently double-reviewed subset and a certified-accountant review spanning every industry--period--difficulty cell and every inconsistency code.
